# Block POV: the format we copy (studied frame by frame from @weirdsecret_)

Source: 5 videos from @weirdsecret_ (QuestionsOvrQuestions, about 44K followers, 3.6M likes). Each one was studied at one frame per second, with its audio transcribed.

## Look
- **Characters:** smooth, flat-shaded box people (not pixel voxels). A cube head about 1.3× body width, with a simple drawn face: dot eyes with white highlights, thin brows, a small nose, and a line mouth that becomes a smile, an open mouth or a frown. Faint blush. Rectangular torso, box arms with box hands, box legs. Clothes are flat colours with tiny details (a tie, a badge, a cross necklace).
- **Worlds:** full, believable places (a Miami pool party with a skyline, hospital rooms, an office floor, stairwells, city streets with a mosque and a church, a rooftop restaurant, a park, a church interior, a cemetery seen from the air). Detailed props with real lighting, shadows, sunsets and night lights.
- **Grade:** cinematic and soft. Shallow depth of field, a warm or cool tint, gentle bloom and slight grain. Video 3 adds a paper or watercolour texture over everything.
- **Camera:** mostly **first person (YOU)** at eye height with **blocky hands** in view, holding a coffee cup, a phone, a tennis racket or a railing. Subtle handheld sway. Cutaways are close-ups on faces, wide shots of crowds, aerials and push-ins. Cuts use **motion-blur whips**. Blinks and closed eyes are black or blurred frames.
- **In-world screens carry story beats:** phone lock screens with dated notifications ("Mom: are you seeing this??"), a breaking-news lower third ("SIGN SEEN OVER EVERY CITY"), ECG monitors, brain scans, signs ("IN CASE OF FIRE…").

## Text layers (all in a 576×1024 frame; scale ×1.875 for 1080×1920)
1. **Title, top center, 0–3 s:** a serif (Garamond-like), white, 2–3 lines. **One key word in colour** (teal for "coma", orange for "16 minutes", gold for "Islam" / "Jesus" / "Christianity"). It fades out by about 3.5 s.
2. **Tracker, left side, about 40% down the frame:**
   - a tiny letter-spaced sans caps label ("SEPT 11, 2001");
   - a big bold sans value ("9:02:56 AM", "DAY 3", "CLOSED", "2,350,000,000");
   - a tiny caps sub-line ("STAIRWELL A · FLOOR 76").
   - The value **ticks live** (a clock every second, a counter counting up, a percentage counting down). It turns **red** in danger, and at the climax it moves to the **center, huge**.
3. **Caption, centered, about 58% down the frame:** an *italic serif*, small, white with a soft shadow. Second person, present tense, 3–9 words: "You jump. Not far enough." / "Your head hits the edge." / "Almost nobody else is going down." Dialogue goes in quotation marks; a quote can carry a tiny caps attribution line. One caption per 2.5–4 s, fading in and out.
4. **Ending:** a black screen with the title centered (serif) and a **gold italic question** under it: "Would you be ready?" / "Would you want them to keep waiting?" / "Would you still need a sign?"

## Sound
- **No narrator.** The captions are read silently.
- **Character voices:** short, emotional lines from people in the scene ("Bro! Jump!", "Can you hear me? It's Mom.", "Relax, it's the other building.", "Sir, the building is secure, go back up.", "They're dead! Take the stairs!").
- **Ambience** fits the place (party, hospital beeps, office alarm, city), with heavy impact sound effects.
- **Emotional music bed:** low piano or strings, or a choir for the religious ones.

## Writing structure (79–99 s, about 22–28 captions)
1. **Open (0–4 s):** the title over a normal, happy moment (a party dare, coffee at the office, dinner, a sunrise crowd). The tracker sets the place and time.
2. **The event (4–15 s):** the thing happens in 2–3 short captions ("Something just hit the North Tower.").
3. **Time jumps:** the tracker advances (DAY 1 → DAY 2 → WEEK 3, or minute by minute). Each jump is a new location or angle.
4. **The real-world anchor:** a real fact or statistic dropped flatly ("Doctors rate you 3 out of 15. The lowest.", "But 4 in 10 times, they're wrong.", "1 in 4 who can't respond can do this.").
5. **Bad advice and dread:** people around you get it wrong ("You may return to your office."), and you choose differently.
6. **Climax:** the tracker goes red or huge, the camera shakes, there's a big sound effect.
7. **Resolution:** a quiet, emotional beat ("You still can't move. But now they know.").
8. **End:** the black card with a gold question aimed at YOU.

## What we copy, what we don't
- **Copy:** the look, the first-person block world, the text system, the tracker, the pacing, voices instead of a narrator, real-anchor facts, and the gold question ending.
- **Don't copy:** their topics as-is. Three of the five are "religion X proven true", and one recreates a real mass-casualty attack from a victim's point of view. Our channel stays neutral on religion and avoids putting viewers inside real tragedies with real victims. Pick **gripping, survivable, real-feeling situations**: trapped in an elevator, lost at sea, waking up during surgery, a whiteout on a mountain, the last flight before a storm, buried in an avalanche, and so on.
